About me

I'm a Research Engineer at BharatGen, working on foundation vision‑language models and the large‑scale systems behind them. Recently that has meant building and training Patram 1.5, our foundation model for document understanding, which took model training, data generation and the infrastructure to bring them together.

I care about both what a model can do and how efficiently we can build and run it at scale, so I move between the two. Before BharatGen I worked on enterprise vision‑language models at Sprinklr and on data‑efficient speech recognition at LTRC, IIIT Hyderabad.

Experience

  1. Sep 2025 – now

    Research Engineer, BharatGen

    Vision team · Hyderabad

    I build BharatGen's document vision‑language models and the systems they're trained on. For Patram 1.5 I built the data programme, a taxonomy of 186 tasks across 12 capabilities filled through large‑scale teacher distillation, and trained the 7B model on 128 GPUs with Hugging Face Trainer and DeepSpeed ZeRO. Before that I first‑authored DocLayout‑VL (ECCV 2026), which reframes layout analysis as open‑set, hierarchical, promptable segmentation. Under the models I built the stack they run on: an evaluation system covering 208 models and 105 benchmarks, a distributed trainer tuned for multi‑node AWS EFA, a crash‑safe, exactly‑once data engine, a per‑node Go storage daemon over local NVMe, and the Kubernetes platform for a 512‑GPU fleet.

  2. May – Jul 2025

    Intern, Sprinklr

    Gurugram

    Sprinklr ran more than ten task‑specific visual engines, for logo detection, OCR, object detection and demographic analysis among others. I designed a single vision‑language model system to replace them. To choose the model I built ViLD, a framework for evaluating open and proprietary VLMs on enterprise data, and showed that with targeted data curation a fine‑tuned Qwen 7B matches Qwen 32B at 80% lower compute. The work was published at AI‑ML Systems 2025.

  3. Apr 2023 – Sep 2025

    Undergraduate Researcher, LTRC

    IIIT Hyderabad · with Prof. Anil Kumar Vuppala

    I worked on data‑efficient speech recognition for low‑resource languages. The question was whether data selection can stand in for model scale, and it can: with cross‑lingual training data chosen by phonetic similarity to the target language, a 300M‑parameter model matches Whisper Large v3 on Persian, Arabic and Urdu. The work produced three papers, at IJCNLP‑AACL 2025, Interspeech 2025 and a COLING 2025 workshop.

  4. Feb – May 2024

    Research Intern, Game Theory

    Hyderabad

    I led the multi‑camera calibration and triangulation pipeline and cut its 3D triangulation error from 30 cm to 3 cm. I also fixed a memory leak in the in‑house annotation tool so it runs at constant memory, and deployed the models to edge devices with ONNX.
